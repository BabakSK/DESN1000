DESN1000 · Impact of flow on effective stress

When water flows, who holds the ground up?

Still water carries part of the ground; flowing water drags on the grains as well. Raise the supply tank and watch the standpipes, the stresses and the grain contacts change together, until the sand boils. Then take the same idea to a real excavation.

Ask the room first, then test it

Where this shows up on site

The rig is the theory. Terzaghi built this exact apparatus to show that the grains carry σ′ = σ − u and nothing else. Upward flow adds γw·i to the pore pressure gradient and takes the same amount from the grains; at icr = γ′/γw ≈ 1 nothing is left and the sand behaves like a heavy liquid. Quicksand is a flow condition, not a kind of sand.

The excavation is the practice. Every basement, pump station or bridge pier dug below the water table inside sheet piles is this problem. The water outside wants to come up through the floor of the pit. If it comes up too fast the floor heaves or boils, the walls lose their toe support and the excavation collapses, usually in minutes. The standard checks are the exit gradient at the floor and Terzaghi's block heave, both computed here from the seepage solution; codes ask for a safety factor of 1.5 to 2.

The same physics on a larger scale. Sand boils at the landward toe of a levee during a flood are upward seepage reaching icr; they are ringed with sandbags to add head, not to stop the water. The 2005 New Orleans floodwall failures and the 2011 Brisbane levee inspections are both stories about this number.
